## Required corrections

### s0 — Exact photographed tread is not verified unchanged today

The narration says the specific print remains today and nothing has smoothed its ridge since 1969. NASA's modern LROC image caption establishes **astronaut trails**, not a present-day close-up proving every ridge of AS11-40-5877 is intact. NASA's photogrammetry discussion that these two prints “survived” refers to later EVA activity and post-EVA photographs from the same mission. Do not turn that into modern tread-level verification. This is a limit of the cited evidence, not evidence that the print is gone. [NASA LROC landing-site image](https://science.nasa.gov/resource/apollo-11-landing-site/), [NASA Apollo 11 photogrammetry](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/alsj/a11/a11Photogrammetry.html).

### s1 — AS11-40-5877 shows a bootprint, not a boot

NASA identifies 5877 as Aldrin's intentionally made soil-mechanics footprint; 5878 is a second view. Frames 5879–5880 show the boot and a second print. Aldrin's attribution and experiment purpose are sound. Change **“Buzz Aldrin's boot” → “Buzz Aldrin's bootprint.”** Keep the archive identifier in a visual credit instead of reading it aloud; it adds no causal information to a one-minute narration. Optional clean replacement for s1:

### s2 — 81,000 years / two centimetres is an estimate, not a measured footprint lifetime

The numbers are supported by the 2016 paper: a secondary-cratering process was estimated to overturn the upper two centimetres on that timescale. NASA explains an extrapolated 99% surface-overturn estimate and a rate over 100 times older micrometeoroid-only models. Say **“estimated”**, retain the important secondary-impact/debris mechanism, and do not assign this exact lifetime to one footprint. [Original paper abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27734864/), [NASA's 2016 research release](https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/earths-moon-hit-by-surprising-number-of-meteoroids/).

Minimal wording change: replace **“image pairs showed … churning”** with **“image pairs led researchers to estimate that impact debris could churn …”**.

Optional replacement that makes the limitation useful to the audience:

> A 2016 study using Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter images estimated that impact debris could churn the top two centimetres of soil in about eighty-one thousand years. That is a surface-mixing estimate, not an expiry date for this footprint.

### s3 — Correct the solar-wind mechanism and the “nothing sweeps sideways” absolute

Solar wind ions alter grain surfaces through irradiation/implantation/sputtering; energetic impacts fracture, melt and redistribute material. Calling radiation and solar wind “grinding” the grains conflates mechanisms. A total absence of sideways transport is also wrong: the same cited study identifies secondary ejecta and a surface-bound jetting process. The distinction needed here is **no atmospheric wind/rain**, not no lateral movement. [NASA lunar-regolith overview, space-weathering discussion](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/05_1_snoble_thelunarregolith.pdf), [Original paper abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27734864/).

Minimal replacement for s3:

> Solar wind and radiation also alter grain surfaces. With no wind or rain to smooth them away, tracks can last for ages. But impacts and their flying debris will eventually erase them. Preserved for now does not mean preserved forever.
